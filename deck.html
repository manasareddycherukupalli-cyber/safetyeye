SafetyEye
The problem

The sites most likely to hurt someone
are the ones with nobody watching.

On a small construction site, factory floor or warehouse, safety is enforced by one supervisor walking around with a clipboard two or three times a shift.

The rest of the time nobody is watching. The violations that cause serious injuries are mundane and continuous — someone crossing into an active machinery zone, a fire exit stacked with material, too many people in a confined space.

Each one is obvious on sight and invisible in the records, because an incident is only logged if a supervisor happened to be standing there.

The fix exists. It doesn't reach them.

Camera-based monitoring means IP cameras, cabling, a DVR or edge server, an internet uplink and a per-camera subscription. That is a capital project — roughly ₹2,00,000 to start.

So the economics invert safety

A contractor running an eight-month site, or a workshop with thirty employees, will never buy it. The workplaces with the worst safety outcomes are precisely the ones with no monitoring at all — not because nobody cares, but because the only product on the market costs more than the business can justify.

What makes it different

Every other system documents the accident.
This one tries to stop it.

Camera-based safety products are forensic. They watch, they log, they report. The worker who crossed into the machine zone finds out they broke a rule when a supervisor reads the report the next day. Nobody gets warned, because a camera wired to a server room cannot speak.

Every CCTV safety system

Person crosses the line → incident recorded → reviewed tomorrow

A record of the near-miss. Useful for the insurer. Useless to the person who nearly got hurt.

SafetyEye

Person heads for the line → phone calls out → they stop

The device tracks how each person is moving, projects their path, and speaks a warning before the breach. A logged breach means the warning failed.

This is only possible because the device is physically there, with a speaker. The property that makes it phone-first is the same property that makes it a different product.

Why workers accept it

The supervisor sees everything.
The paperwork sees nobody.

Tell a workforce there is a camera watching them and the video goes to a vendor's servers, and you get resistance — reasonably. Adoption, not accuracy, is what kills safety technology on real sites.

SafetyEye runs the model on the phone's own processor. There is no server on the other end. The claim that footage never leaves the device is not a policy promise — it is an architectural fact.

And the log and the report have different audiences. The supervisor is on site and needs to act, so they see faces. The client, auditor or insurer needs to know that a zone was breached four times on Tuesday, not who.

On this phone — faces visible

Live view and the on-device alert log keep clear frames. You cannot intervene in real time if you cannot tell who to call out to.

Leaving the phone — faces blurred

The exported compliance report anonymises faces on-device, by default. Unedited frames stay with the supervisor and can be produced if an incident is formally investigated.

Nothing is uploaded, ever

No video is transmitted or stored. Only the still frame of the moment a rule was broken is kept — on the device. Turn the network off and it behaves identically.

How it works

Working prototype, built and running today.

  • Open-source model, on-device. COCO-SSD (MobileNet backbone) via TensorFlow.js, loaded once and executed entirely in the device runtime. No inference server, no API key.
  • A rules layer on a general detector. The model finds people and objects; the safety logic — zone geometry, occupancy, obstruction — is ours. This is how you ship without a labelled dataset.
  • Multi-object tracker with velocity. Detections are matched frame to frame and eased, giving each person a smoothed trajectory. That trajectory is what makes the pre-breach warning possible.
  • Decoupled inference and render loops. Detection runs to a target rate while the overlay draws at 60fps against interpolated boxes — throttled, because a monitor must last a whole shift.
  • Persistent shift log. Zones, settings and alerts with photographs survive a reload or the phone locking. A record that evaporates is not a record.
  • Built for the deployment. Screen wake lock so a propped phone doesn't sleep, haptics, spoken alerts, and correct behaviour when the network drops.

Honest scope

The detector recognises people and objects, not hard hats. Today it enforces zone intrusion, occupancy and blocked egress. PPE detection is the same pipeline with a fine-tuned model swapped in — the architecture is deliberately model-agnostic.

Why a web runtime was the right call

55% of the build weekend is Red Light — phone only. A native Android app needs a laptop toolchain and would stall for eleven hours. A self-contained web build can be edited and run entirely on the device, and ports to any phone on any site.

What the 30 hours are for

Moving inference onto the Snapdragon NPU, a PPE-trained detector, and multi-phone zone coverage reporting into one supervisor device.

Three zones covered by refurbished handsets, against ₹2,00,000+ for a wired install with a recurring subscription — and no cabling, no electrician.

0
Monthly cost

No cloud inference, no per-camera licence. The economics work for a site that exists for eight months and then moves.

Roadmap

  • PPE detection — hard hats, high-vis, gloves, via a fine-tuned detector on the same pipeline.
  • Multi-phone fleet — several devices each watching a zone, rolling up to one supervisor handset over the local network. Still no cloud.
  • Pattern reporting — "most breaches occur 14:00–15:00, just after lunch." Logging becomes insight.

Who it is for

Small and mid-sized contractors, fabrication workshops, warehouses and commercial kitchens — operations of roughly 20 to 200 people that are legally responsible for safety records but have no monitoring budget. In India that is the overwhelming majority of workplaces where serious injuries occur.

The buyer is the site supervisor, not a procurement committee. That is why it has to install in thirty seconds and cost nothing per month.
